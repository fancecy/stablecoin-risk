Stablecoin depegging risk prediction and reserve stress testing

Six episodes when a stablecoin drifted from one dollar, and a balance-sheet stress test of how USDC's issuer could have met redemptions when its cash was frozen at Silicon Valley Bank.

Recovery here means clearing the redemption backlog, not restoring the market price. Results are conditional simulations, not causal estimates. Methods and limitations

    Reserve stress test: USDC, March 2023

    The same redemption requests hit the same February 2023 balance sheet. Four responses are compared over 30 days. All 300 settings were computed in advance by one ledger model, and nothing is interpolated.

    Responses to compare
    Fixed assumptions

    60% of baseline net cash is frozen and released the day after the freeze ends. No Treasury price shock, a 1% liquidation haircut, and a 30-day horizon. Requests arrive over five days, weighted 40, 25, 15, 10 and 10%.

    The cash buffer targets 30% of initial liabilities, is built 30 days before the shock at a 4% annual opportunity spread, and sits outside the frozen bank.

    Credit is capped at 10% of initial liabilities, costs 8% a year plus a 0.5% commitment fee, and needs collateral worth 110% of principal. Nothing is repaid within the horizon.

    The sales cap applies to original Treasury holdings, and only unpledged Treasuries can be sold.

    Scenario assumptions and research scope

    The 20% redemption shock, 60% cash-freeze fraction, financing rates and sales limits are model assumptions, not verified historical observations from Circle or Tether. Simulation outputs come from our code.

    Earlier prediction experiments remain part of the research record; further work focuses on stress testing and intervention comparisons after depegging. CoinGecko was a proposed source but is not an input in the current study. CoinMarketCap comparison points in team-provided figures lack matching download records and are not listed as an integrated dataset.

    How to read these results

    This is a balance-sheet simulation under stated assumptions. It is not a price forecast or a causal policy evaluation.

    When the reserve report was published is unverified, and the issuer's rights to sell or pledge the Treasuries are not established. Confidence feedback, self-reinforcing runs and insolvency resolution are not modelled.
